Apart from these, a few notable projects from my time at uni includes:

An AI language model. It was a toy example trained on some public domain books. It would, at best, say 10-15 unique words before being stuck in a loop. This sounds terrible, but is quite reasonable given the available computational resources and training data. GPT1, the precursor to the precursor to the first version of ChatGPT, was trained on 8 high-end CPUs over a month. We had a deadline of a couple weeks to design, program and implement a model from scratch. Ours was trained on 1 CPU for an hour. But the purpose of our model was to learn the key principles of training a language model, and in that regard, it was a success.

Image recognition on the CIFAR-10 dataset. I made a model meant to identify whether or not an image contained a dog. It hit about an 80-90% accuracy on images from the CIFAR-10 dataset(altered to be approx. 50/50 dog and non-dog), but only 1 of the 8 photos of my dog that i gave it was correctly identified. So the model reguralized very poorly when encountering new data. But it was a relatively simple Convolutional Neural Network, trained on limited data. The important thing is that I understand how CNNs work, and that I could excel under the right guidance.
